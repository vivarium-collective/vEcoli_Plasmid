================================================================================
DnaG (DNA Primase) Expression Investigation Summary
vEcoli model — April 2026
================================================================================

BACKGROUND
----------
During multigeneration simulations (plasmid branch), and without plasmids, DnaG (DNA primase,
EG10239-MONOMER) was observed at counts of ~1 molecule/cell, causing replication
initiation failures. DnaG requires 2 copies per oriC for replisome assembly.
This document traces the origin of the DnaG expression problem through the
model's data pipeline.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. EXPERIMENTAL PROTEIN COUNTS (what biology says)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DnaG is a genuinely low-copy protein, but not as low as the simulation produces.

Schmidt 2015 (schmidt2015_javier_table.tsv) — absolute protein counts per cell:
  Glucose (minimal-like):      6 molecules/cell
  LB (rich media):            14 molecules/cell
  Glycerol + AA:               8 molecules/cell
  Acetate:                     4 molecules/cell   <- lowest observed
  Fumarate:                    9 molecules/cell
  Glycerol:                   10 molecules/cell
  Chemostat µ=0.5:            16 molecules/cell
  Chemostat µ=0.12:           16 molecules/cell
  Stationary phase:            5 molecules/cell
  Range across all conditions: 4–19, mean ≈ 10.7

Wisniewski 2014 (wisniewski2014_supp2.tsv) — 3 replicates:
  Rep 1: 13.3,  Rep 2: 18.6,  Rep 3: 29.6
  Mean ≈ 20.5 molecules/cell

Houser 2015 (houser2015_javier_table.tsv) — time-series samples:
  Mean ≈ 11.6,  range: 2–28 molecules/cell

Cross-dataset summary:
  Overall mean:  ~12 molecules/cell
  Overall range:  2–30 molecules/cell
  Simulation:    ~1 molecule/cell
  Deficit:       ~10–12x too low


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE DATA PIPELINE (how fold changes are derived and fed into the model)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1 — EcoCyc regulatory topology (external, manually downloaded)
  File: reconstruction/ecoli/scripts/nca/data/ecocyc/regulation.tsv
  Source: EcoCyc v24.1
  Content: Known TF→gene regulatory links with direction (+/-/NIL)
  Role: Structural PRIOR for NCA — defines which TF-gene pairs exist and
        whether they are activating or repressing.
  For lexA→dnaG: EcoCyc records direction "-" (repression).
  Note: EcoCyc is the sole source of the lexA→dnaG relationship.
        EcoMAC does NOT discover this link; it only quantifies its magnitude.

STEP 2 — EcoMAC expression compendium (external)
  File: reconstruction/ecoli/scripts/nca/data/compendium/seq/EcoMAC.tsv
  Source: Carrera et al. 2014, Molecular Systems Biology
  Content: 4,189 genes × 2,198 conditions, log2 microarray expression values
           (no row/column headers in the file itself)
  Row index: gene_names.tsv (row index → b-number → gene symbol)
  Column index: samples.tsv (column index → full experimental metadata)
  dnaG is row 3442 (0-indexed: 3441)

  dnaG expression statistics across all 2,198 conditions:
    mean = 10.52 log2
    std  =  0.63 log2
    min  =  8.22 log2 (plain LB, no perturbation)
    max  = 13.10 log2 (biofilm condition)

  Key finding — DNA damage conditions in EcoMAC:
    Norfloxacin (SOS-inducing, cols 325–374):  mean = 10.99 log2 (+0.47 above mean)
    UV irradiation (col 547):                  10.45 log2 (barely above mean)
    Norfloxacin appears in 7 of the top 20 highest dnaG conditions.

  Interpretation: dnaG is upregulated in norfloxacin (SOS) conditions,
  consistent with lexA de-repression. However, this means the NCA fold
  change magnitude is being driven by the SOS response contrast, not by
  natural variation in lexA activity during normal growth.

STEP 3 — NCA computation (fold_changes.py)
  Script: reconstruction/ecoli/scripts/nca/fold_changes.py
  Method: Network Component Analysis
    - Factorizes E ≈ A · P, where E = expression matrix (genes × conditions),
      A = regulatory weights (genes × TFs), P = TF activity (TFs × conditions)
    - A is sign-constrained by EcoCyc: lexA→dnaG entry is forced negative
    - calculate_fold_changes() bins conditions into "high lexA activity" vs
      "low lexA activity" (±1 std, minimum 10 conditions each side)
    - FC = A[dnaG, lexA] × (mean_high_P − mean_low_P)
    - Only entries where NCA sign agrees with EcoCyc direction are kept

  Result for lexA→dnaG:
    log2 fold change = -1.71  (i.e., 2^1.71 = 3.27x repression)

  Critical caveat: The "low lexA activity" conditions are dominated by
  SOS/DNA damage experiments (norfloxacin) where lexA is cleaved by RecA.
  This is the full SOS de-repression signal, not gradual variation in
  lexA binding during normal growth.

STEP 4 — Flat files loaded into the model
  reconstruction/ecoli/flat/fold_changes_nca.tsv  <- NCA output (Step 3)
  reconstruction/ecoli/flat/fold_changes.tsv       <- manually curated literature values
  reconstruction/ecoli/flat/condition/tf_condition.tsv  <- TF type and conditions

  lexA in tf_condition.tsv:
    TF type:            "0CS" (zero-component system)
    Active condition:   minimal media (lexA bound, repressing)
    Inactive condition: minimal media + {EG10533_RNA: 0} (lexA knockout)

  What "0CS" means in the model (fit_sim_data_1.py:3920):
    p_promoter_bound = min(1, TF_count / n_promoter_targets)
    i.e., if lexA molecules exist, the promoter is treated as always bound.
    LexA is NEVER inactivated during normal simulation — only in the artificial
    parca fitting step is an "inactive" condition considered.

  dnaG fold change entries:
    fold_changes_nca.tsv:  lexA → dnaG  =  -1.71 log2
    fold_changes.tsv:      lexA → dnaG  =  NOT PRESENT (not in curated set)

STEP 5 — simulation_data.py merges both fold change files
  Both fold_changes.tsv and fold_changes_nca.tsv are loaded in sequence.
  Result stored as: sim_data.tf_to_fold_change[tf][gene] = 2^FC (linear)
  For dnaG: sim_data.tf_to_fold_change["PC00010"]["EG10239_RNA"] = 2^-1.71 = 0.306

STEP 6 — Parca fitting (fit_sim_data_1.py)
  calculatePromoterBoundProbability():
    For lexA (0CS): p_bound ≈ 1 always (if molecules exist)

  The fitting solves a linear system G·r = k, where:
    G = matrix of p_bound values per (RNA, TF, condition)
    k = vector of expression targets per (RNA, condition)
    r = solution vector containing basal_prob (alpha) and delta_prob per TF

  For dnaG with lexA FC = -1.71:
    lexA_active condition:   r_alpha + 1 × r_lexA = X        (observed basal)
    lexA_inactive condition: r_alpha + 0 × r_lexA = 3.27 × X (lexA knockout)
    => r_alpha = 3.27X,  r_lexA = -2.27X

  At runtime: basal_prob + delta_prob × p_bound = 3.27X + (-2.27X) × 1 = X
  In principle the fold change cancels for 0CS. In practice, large compensating
  terms (3.27X and -2.27X) can amplify numerical errors and fitting artifacts,
  especially for stochastically low-copy genes.

  Output stored in sim_data:
    transcription_regulation.basal_prob   — per-TU basal transcription probability
    transcription_regulation.delta_prob   — sparse matrix of TF contributions

STEP 7 — Runtime simulation
  Each timestep: p_transcription[dnaG] = basal_prob[dnaG] + delta_prob[dnaG, lexA]
  Since lexA is 0CS and always bound, lexA repression is always applied.
  The SOS de-repression (lexA cleavage by RecA) never occurs — there is no
  DNA damage response in the current model.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. COMPOUNDING FACTORS SUPPRESSING DnaG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three independent factors multiply to suppress DnaG protein counts:

Factor 1 — lexA fold change: -1.71 log2 (3.27x transcript reduction)
  Source: NCA on EcoMAC (fold_changes_nca.tsv)
  Problem: Derived from SOS/DNA damage conditions (norfloxacin) where lexA is
           fully cleaved. Represents the complete SOS de-repression contrast,
           not the natural range of lexA activity during normal growth.
           LexA is always active in the model (0CS), so this state is never
           reversed at runtime. The magnitude is likely inflated.
  Note:    This fold change is only in fold_changes_nca.tsv — it was NOT
           included in the manually curated fold_changes.tsv, which may
           indicate prior awareness that it is uncertain.

Factor 2 — ppGpp fold change: -2.46 log2 (5.50x transcript reduction)
  Source: Sanchez-Vazquez et al. 2019 PNAS (ppgpp_fc.tsv, "1+2+ 5 min" column)
  Content: Genome-wide effect of ppGpp binding to both sites on RNA polymerase
  dnaG entry: -2.46 log2 at 5 min, -2.91 log2 at 10 min after ppGpp induction
  This is applied in the "with_aa" condition and ppGpp-regulated growth states.

Factor 3 — Translation efficiency: 0.11
  Source: Li et al. 2014 Cell, Table S4 (translation_efficiency.tsv)
         "Quantifying Absolute Protein Synthesis Rates Reveals Principles
          Underlying Allocation of Cellular Resources"
  Method: Ribosome profiling (ribosome footprint density / mRNA abundance)
  dnaG TE = 0.11 vs. dataset mean = 1.11, median = 0.93
  Percentile: 3rd percentile out of 2,387 genes with data
  Meaning: dnaG mRNA is translated ~10x less efficiently than the median gene
  Note: This is a direct experimental measurement and likely trustworthy,
        consistent with DnaG being a genuinely low-copy protein.

Combined effect (rough estimate, multiplicative):
  If baseline gives ~12 molecules (observed), applying:
    3.27x (lexA) × any fitting artifacts → could push to ~3–4
    + stochastic noise at low copy numbers → can reach 0–1
  The TE is already baked into the observed counts, so it's not additive here,
  but the inflated lexA fold change is the most likely source of the remaining
  ~10–12x deficit between simulation (~1) and observation (~6–20).

dnaG operon context:
  Gene is part of the rpsU-dnaG-rpoD operon:
    rpsU = 30S ribosomal protein S21 (essential, highly expressed)
    dnaG = DNA primase (this gene)
    rpoD = sigma70 (essential, highly expressed)
  It is biologically implausible that this operon has bottom 3rd percentile
  translation efficiency. The Li et al. TE measurement may be worth
  cross-checking, though it is a direct experimental value.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. KEY BIOLOGICAL CONTEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LexA biology:
  - LexA is the master repressor of the SOS response in E. coli
  - Under normal growth (no DNA damage): lexA is constitutively active,
    always bound to SOS boxes, always repressing its targets
  - Under DNA damage: RecA becomes activated as a coprotease and cleaves lexA,
    de-repressing all SOS genes (including dnaG, recA, umuC, sulA, etc.)
  - The WCM simulates normal growth only — no DNA damage, no RecA activation,
    no lexA cleavage

LexA in the model:
  - Type: 0CS (zero-component system)
  - p_bound ≈ 1 always during simulation
  - 54 target genes in fold_changes_nca.tsv (all with negative fold changes)
  - Active condition = inactive condition = minimal media (only difference is
    lexA RNA set to 0 in the "inactive" case — i.e., a knockout)

DnaG biology:
  - DNA primase: synthesises RNA primers for Okazaki fragments and replication
    initiation
  - Required for both chromosome and plasmid replication
  - Required count: 2 per oriC for replisome assembly
  - Genuinely a low-copy protein (~6–20 molecules/cell across growth conditions)
  - Being in the rpsU-dnaG-rpoD operon means its promoter drives expression of
    two other essential, high-abundance genes — severe repression of this
    operon would be deleterious

EcoMAC and lexA/dnaG:
  - EcoCyc provides the regulatory link (lexA represses dnaG)
  - EcoMAC provides the magnitude (NCA estimates -1.71 log2)
  - dnaG is elevated in norfloxacin conditions (top 20 highest expression)
    which is consistent with SOS de-repression, confirming the regulation is real
  - But the fold change magnitude comes from the full SOS contrast (lexA fully
    cleaved), not from natural variation in lexA occupancy during normal growth


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. SUMMARY OF THE PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The simulation produces ~1 DnaG/cell vs the observed ~6–20/cell across all
experimental proteomics datasets. The most likely cause is the NCA-derived
lexA fold change of -1.71 log2 (from fold_changes_nca.tsv), which:

  1. Was computed from SOS-inducing conditions (norfloxacin) in EcoMAC, where
     lexA is fully cleaved — representing the maximum possible de-repression,
     not the natural regulatory range during normal growth.

  2. Is only in fold_changes_nca.tsv (not in the manually curated
     fold_changes.tsv), meaning it was not previously validated by curation.

  3. Feeds into the parca fitting as large compensating terms (r_alpha = 3.27X,
     r_lexA = -2.27X) which can amplify stochastic errors for low-copy genes.

  4. Is applied permanently at runtime (lexA is always bound, 0CS) but the
     compensating "inactive" state (lexA knockout) never occurs in simulation.

  5. Compounds with an already-low translation efficiency (TE = 0.11, 3rd
     percentile) measured by Li et al. 2014.

The lexA regulation of dnaG is real (EcoCyc annotation, confirmed by EcoMAC
expression pattern in SOS conditions). The problem is the magnitude of the
fold change and how it was derived, not the existence of the regulatory link.

  4. The fold change is applied at the CISTRON level, meaning it should apply
     to all TUs containing dnaG. However, investigation of the actual parca
     output reveals a deeper problem: TU00434 and TU00435 are absent from
     sim_data entirely. The model deduplicates TUs by gene content
     (getter_functions.py:_build_rna_sequences), and since TU00352, TU00434,
     and TU00435 all contain the same three genes, only TU00352 (the first one
     encountered) is retained. TU00434 and TU00435 — the lexA-independent
     promoters — are silently dropped. This means dnaG has only ONE TU in the
     model, and that TU carries the lexA fold change and has rna_synth_prob of
     only 0.0000884 in the basal condition. By contrast, rpsU has a dedicated
     single-gene TU (TU00472, synth_prob 0.0033) giving it ~38x higher
     expression than dnaG from the shared operon TU alone. dnaG has no such
     fallback. The deduplication logic conflates genuinely duplicate TU entries
     (same transcript, slightly different mapped boundaries) with biologically
     distinct alternative promoters for the same operon. This is the dominant
     cause of the observed ~10–12x DnaG protein deficit.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. rpsUp2 — LEXA-INDEPENDENT PROMOTER (additional compounding error)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The rpsU-dnaG-rpoD operon has THREE transcription units in the model, each with
a different transcription start site (TSS) corresponding to different promoters:

  TU00352   rpsU-dnaG-rpoD   TSS = 3,210,646
  TU00434   rpsU-dnaG-rpoD   TSS = 3,210,712
  TU00435   rpsU-dnaG-rpoD   TSS = 3,210,735
  TU00472   rpsU only        TSS = 3,210,646

LexA operator position (EcoCyc): 3,210,729 – 3,210,748 (+ strand)

Mapping the operator against each TSS:

  TU00352  TSS 3,210,646  operator is 83 bp DOWNSTREAM of TSS  -> NOT blocked
  TU00434  TSS 3,210,712  operator is 17 bp DOWNSTREAM of TSS  -> NOT blocked
  TU00435  TSS 3,210,735  operator is  6 bp UPSTREAM of TSS    -> BLOCKED
  TU00472  TSS 3,210,646  operator is 83 bp DOWNSTREAM of TSS  -> NOT blocked

For a repressor like lexA to block a promoter, the operator must overlap with
the promoter elements (-10/-35 boxes) or the TSS. When the TSS is upstream of
the operator, RNAP has already initiated transcription before reaching the
operator — the promoter is constitutively active regardless of lexA.

  TU00435 (~rpsUp3):  BLOCKED by lexA. Operator overlaps the -10 box (~3,210,725)
                      and TSS (3,210,735). This is the lexA-regulated promoter.
  TU00352 (~rpsUp1):  NOT blocked. TSS is 83 bp upstream of the operator.
  TU00434 (~rpsUp2):  NOT blocked. TSS is 17 bp upstream of the operator.
                      This is the "rpsUp2" promoter the user identified as
                      lexA-independent.

How the model incorrectly handles this:

  The fold change pipeline applies the lexA FC at the CISTRON level (EG10239).
  In simulation_data.py, the target maps to EG10239_RNA, and in the parca
  (fit_sim_data_1.py:3873), cistron_id_to_rna_indexes("EG10239") returns ALL
  THREE TUs containing dnaG. The lexA fold change of -1.71 log2 is therefore
  applied to TU00352, TU00434, and TU00435 — including the two that are
  constitutively active and should not be repressed by lexA at all.

  In reality, only TU00435 should carry the lexA fold change.
  TU00352 and TU00434 are lexA-independent and should be constitutively expressed.

Combined effect of this error:
  The model suppresses 2/3 of the dnaG-producing TUs that should be unaffected
  by lexA, on top of an already-inflated fold change magnitude (derived from SOS
  conditions). This is the most likely dominant cause of the ~10–12x DnaG
  protein deficit observed in simulation.

Also note: EcoCyc records lexA as also repressing rpsU (EG10920), and
fold_changes_nca.tsv has "lexA" → "rpsU" = -1.89 log2. The same incorrect
TU-level application likely affects rpsU expression as well, since TU00352,
TU00434, and TU00472 all contain rpsU and have TSSs upstream of the operator.

Additional finding — TU00434 and TU00435 are absent from sim_data entirely:
  Checking the actual parca-built sim_data confirms that only TU00352 is
  present in rna_data for the dnaG cistron. TU00434 and TU00435 fail
  is_valid_molecule() because they have no sequence and no mass entry.

  Root cause (getter_functions.py:_build_rna_sequences, lines 261-270):
  The model deduplicates transcription units by their gene content. It builds
  a gene_tuple = sorted(genes_in_TU) for each TU. If a gene_tuple has already
  been seen, the TU is silently skipped — only its evidence codes are merged
  into the first TU. Since TU00352, TU00434, and TU00435 all contain the same
  three genes (EG10920/rpsU, EG10239/dnaG, EG10896/rpoD), and TU00352 appears
  first
  (lowest TU ID), TU00434 and TU00435 are dropped entirely.

  This deduplication was designed to handle genuinely duplicate TU entries in
  EcoCyc (the same transcript listed twice with slightly different boundaries).
  However, it incorrectly collapses BIOLOGICALLY DISTINCT PROMOTERS for the
  same operon. TU00352, TU00434, and TU00435 are not duplicates — they are
  three separate transcription initiation sites (rpsUp1, rpsUp2, rpsUp3)
  driving the same downstream genes from different promoters, only one of which
  (TU00435) is regulated by lexA.

  Consequence: dnaG has only one TU in the model (TU00352), which carries the
  lexA fold change and has rna_synth_prob = 0.0000884 in the basal condition.
  rpsU and rpoD both have dedicated single-gene TUs (TU00472 and TU0-14460
  respectively) that give them much higher expression independently of TU00352.
  dnaG has no such fallback — it is entirely dependent on TU00352.

  Confirmed rna_synth_prob values in basal condition:
    dnaG  (TU00352 only):          0.0000884
    rpsU  (TU00472, primary TU):   0.0033321  (~38x higher than dnaG)
    rpoD  (TU0-14460, primary TU): 0.0000995
    recA  (TU00062, primary TU):   0.0001001

  In the lexA-KO condition (PC00010__inactive):
    dnaG: 0.000265  (3x higher — confirming lexA is suppressing TU00352)
    rpsU (TU00472): 0.002900 (unchanged, independent of lexA)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. SYSTEMIC SCOPE OF THE TU-LEVEL MISAPPLICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The dnaG/rpsU case is not an isolated error — it reflects a fundamental
architectural limitation of how fold changes are applied in the model.

The fold change pipeline applies regulation at the CISTRON level (TF → gene
name). In simulation_data.py, each fold change target maps to a single RNA ID
(gene_symbol_to_rna_id[gene]), and in the parca, cistron_id_to_rna_indexes()
returns ALL transcription units containing that cistron. The fold change is then
applied uniformly to every TU, regardless of whether that TU's promoter is
actually accessible to the TF's binding site.

In reality, a TF binding site at a specific genomic position can only block
promoters whose TSS is downstream of (or overlapping) the operator. Any TU
whose TSS is upstream of the binding site has already initiated transcription
before RNAP reaches the operator — that promoter is unaffected by the TF.

Scale of the problem:
  Total genes in the model with fold changes AND multiple TUs at
  different transcription start sites: 773 genes

  For many of these, the TSS differences are small (a few bp, likely the same
  promoter with slightly different mapped start positions) and the error is
  minor. But for any gene where one or more TUs have TSSs genuinely upstream
  of the TF operator — as is the case for dnaG/rpsU — the model incorrectly
  suppresses constitutively active promoters that should be unaffected.

What a correct implementation would require:
  1. Storing the genomic position of each TF operator/binding site alongside
     the fold change data (not currently in any flat file)
  2. Applying fold changes at the TU level: for each TF-gene pair, determine
     which specific TUs have TSSs downstream of the operator, and apply the
     fold change only to those TUs
  3. Restructuring fold_changes.tsv and fold_changes_nca.tsv to specify TU IDs
     rather than (or in addition to) gene names

This is a non-trivial architectural refactor affecting potentially hundreds of
genes. The dnaG case is the most immediately impactful discovered so far because
the constitutive TUs (TU00352, TU00434) represent 2/3 of dnaG's transcriptional
output and the incorrectly applied fold change has a measurable effect on cell
viability (replication initiation failures in multigeneration simulations).

Next steps to consider (to be discussed separately)
================================================================================

SHORT-TERM (dnaG-specific):
- A variant (ecoli/variants/tf_tu_exemption.py) has been created and is
  currently being run (experiment: multigen_ecoli_dnag_fix, variant 1).
  It removes lexA's delta_prob contribution from TU00352 and absorbs it into
  basal_prob, making TU00352 constitutively expressed. This is the best fix
  achievable within the current parca infrastructure (see Limitation below).
- Remove lexA→dnaG and lexA→rpsU entries from fold_changes_nca.tsv: the fold
  change magnitude is inflated from SOS conditions, was never in the manually
  curated fold_changes.tsv, and only TU00435 (currently absent) should carry it.
  Removing these entries would make dnaG constitutive at the parca level,
  without needing the post-parca variant.
- Cross-check dnaG translation efficiency (TE=0.11, Li et al. 2014) against
  Schmidt 2015 and other proteomics datasets for consistency.

KNOWN LIMITATION — Restoring TU00434 breaks parca:
  Modifying getter_functions.py:_build_rna_sequences to preserve TUs with
  different start positions (alternative promoters) was attempted. It was
  reverted because the parca solver (fitPromoterBoundProbability, ECOS) fails
  with RuntimeError("Solver could not find optimal value") when multiple TUs
  share the same gene content.

  Root cause of the parca failure: parca assigns expression targets (the k
  vector in the G·r = k linear system) at the gene level, then maps them to
  TUs. When TU00352, TU00434, and TU00435 all share the same three genes, each
  gets the same expression target. The solver then tries to satisfy this target
  three times simultaneously within [0,1] probability constraints — the system
  becomes overconstrained and infeasible. The parca has no mechanism to split a
  gene's observed expression level across alternative promoters, because it has
  only gene-level (not TU-level) expression data.

  What a complete fix would require (Layer 2 — medium term):
  - Modify build_vector_k and build_matrix_G in fit_sim_data_1.py to handle
    co-TUs (multiple TUs sharing the same gene set) by distributing the
    expression target among them (e.g. equally, or weighted by promoter
    strength if data is available) rather than assigning the full target
    to each TU independently.
  - This would allow TU00434 to exist as a separate constitutive TU alongside
    TU00352 and TU00435, with the total expression correctly summing to the
    observed gene-level expression.
  - Only then can the fold change be correctly restricted to TU00435 (the
    lexA-regulated promoter) while TU00352 and TU00434 remain constitutive.

MEDIUM-TERM (0CS TFs and TU deduplication scope):
- Audit all 0CS TFs for the same pattern: inflated fold changes derived from
  conditions where the TF is artificially inactivated rather than from natural
  regulatory variation during normal growth.
- Audit the TU deduplication logic more broadly: any operon with multiple
  experimentally confirmed promoters (different TSS positions for the same
  gene set) may have the same issue as dnaG. Fixing this requires the parca
  co-TU expression splitting described above.

LONG-TERM (architectural):
- Redesign fold change application to be TU-level rather than cistron-level.
- Store genomic positions of TF operator sites alongside fold change data.
- For each TF-gene pair, apply the fold change only to TUs whose TSS is
  downstream of the operator (i.e., actually blocked by the TF).
- This affects 773 genes in the current model and is a non-trivial refactor,
  but is necessary for correct regulatory representation.
- For lexA and SOS genes specifically: consider replacing negative fold changes
  with a positive (de-repression) fold change applied only in a future DNA
  damage / SOS response condition.
